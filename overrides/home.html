freqprob

Frequency-based probability estimation with smoothing — a small, well-tested Python library.

v0.8.1·DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23917394·MIT·Python 3.10–3.12·GitHub·PyPI
Tutorial Concepts Methods Evaluation How-to guides API References Cite

The central difficulty in working with frequency counts is the unobserved: how much probability to assign to events that never appeared in the data. FreqProb converts counts into smoothed probability estimates that address this directly — reserving a principled share of mass for what was not seen — and offers a range of classical smoothing methods, from Laplace and Lidstone to Simple Good-Turing and Kneser-Ney, behind a single, consistent interface.

Quick start

>>> import freqprob
>>> counts = {"the": 100, "cat": 50, "dog": 30, "bird": 10}
>>> freqprob.MLE(counts, logprob=False)("elephant")
0.0
>>> laplace = freqprob.Laplace(counts, bins=10_000,
...                            logprob=False)
>>> round(laplace("cat"), 4)  # observed
0.005
>>> round(laplace("elephant"), 4)  # unseen
0.0001

A raw relative-frequency estimate gives the unseen "elephant" probability 0, which breaks any model that multiplies probabilities or takes their logarithm. Smoothing reserves some mass for what has not been seen yet; every estimator is built from counts and called the same way, so swapping methods does not change the call site.

Beyond text

The problem — estimating probabilities from counts, including for the unobserved — recurs across fields. FreqProb is domain-neutral:

  • Text. n-gram and word-frequency models; term weighting.
  • Ecology. species-abundance data and the classical unseen-species problem.
  • Genomics. k-mer frequencies over the space of possible subsequences.
  • Categorical data. smoothed category priors for features and event counts.

Related projects

  • nltk.probability. several of the same estimators (Laplace, Lidstone, ELE, Simple Good–Turing, Kneser–Ney, Witten–Bell) within a broader NLP toolkit; FreqProb is standalone, works on any frequency counts, and is tested against NLTK.
  • ASymCat. uses FreqProb for smoothing and bias-corrected entropy in its directional measures of association between categorical variables.
