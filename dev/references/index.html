    References¶

    FreqProb implements established methods from the statistics, natural language processing and ecology literatures. This page lists the primary sources for each method, so that work using FreqProb can cite the methods as well as the software. All entries are also available as a BibTeX file: references.bib.

    To cite FreqProb itself, see How to cite.

    Additive smoothing¶

    Laplace, Lidstone and ELE add a pseudo-count to every possible outcome: 1 for Laplace, an arbitrary gamma for Lidstone, and 0.5 for the Expected Likelihood Estimate.

    • Laplace, P.-S. (1814). Essai philosophique sur les probabilités. Paris: Courcier.
    • Lidstone, G. J. (1920). Note on the general case of the Bayes–Laplace formula for inductive or a posteriori probabilities. Transactions of the Faculty of Actuaries, 8, 182–192.
    • Manning, C. D., & Schütze, H. (1999). Foundations of Statistical Natural Language Processing. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press. (Section 6.2 covers Laplace, Lidstone and the ELE.)

    Good–Turing and Witten–Bell¶

    SimpleGoodTuring follows Gale and Sampson's simplification of the Good–Turing estimator, and sample_coverage is the Good–Turing estimate of sample coverage. WittenBell reserves mass in proportion to the number of distinct types seen.

    • Good, I. J. (1953). The population frequencies of species and the estimation of population parameters. Biometrika, 40(3–4), 237–264. doi:10.1093/biomet/40.3-4.237
    • Gale, W. A., & Sampson, G. (1995). Good–Turing frequency estimation without tears. Journal of Quantitative Linguistics, 2(3), 217–237. doi:10.1080/09296179508590051
    • Witten, I. H., & Bell, T. C. (1991). The zero-frequency problem: Estimating the probabilities of novel events in adaptive text compression. IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, 37(4), 1085–1094. doi:10.1109/18.87000

    Discounting and back-off for n-grams¶

    AbsoluteDiscounting, KneserNey, ModifiedKneserNey, PitmanYor, KatzBackoff and StupidBackoff.

    • Ney, H., Essen, U., & Kneser, R. (1994). On structuring probabilistic dependences in stochastic language modelling. Computer Speech & Language, 8(1), 1–38. doi:10.1006/csla.1994.1001 (absolute discounting)
    • Kneser, R., & Ney, H. (1995). Improved backing-off for M-gram language modeling. In Proceedings of ICASSP-95 (Vol. 1, pp. 181–184). doi:10.1109/ICASSP.1995.479394 (Kneser–Ney)
    • Chen, S. F., & Goodman, J. (1999). An empirical study of smoothing techniques for language modeling. Computer Speech & Language, 13(4), 359–393. doi:10.1006/csla.1999.0128 (modified Kneser–Ney, and a comparison of most methods here)
    • Pitman, J., & Yor, M. (1997). The two-parameter Poisson–Dirichlet distribution derived from a stable subordinator. The Annals of Probability, 25(2), 855–900. doi:10.1214/aop/1024404422
    • Teh, Y. W. (2006). A hierarchical Bayesian language model based on Pitman–Yor processes. In Proceedings of COLING/ACL 2006 (pp. 985–992). doi:10.3115/1220175.1220299 (Pitman–Yor smoothing for language models)
    • Katz, S. M. (1987). Estimation of probabilities from sparse data for the language model component of a speech recognizer. IEEE Transactions on Acoustics, Speech, and Signal Processing, 35(3), 400–401. doi:10.1109/TASSP.1987.1165125
    • Brants, T., Popat, A. C., Xu, P., Och, F. J., & Dean, J. (2007). Large language models in machine translation. In Proceedings of EMNLP-CoNLL 2007 (pp. 858–867). ACL Anthology D07-1090 (Stupid Back-off)

    Interpolation and Bayesian smoothing¶

    Interpolated, JelinekMercer and Bayesian.

    • Jelinek, F., & Mercer, R. L. (1980). Interpolated estimation of Markov source parameters from sparse data. In E. S. Gelsema & L. N. Kanal (Eds.), Pattern Recognition in Practice (pp. 381–397). Amsterdam: North-Holland.
    • MacKay, D. J. C., & Peto, L. C. B. (1995). A hierarchical Dirichlet language model. Natural Language Engineering, 1(3), 289–308. doi:10.1017/S1351324900000218

    Entropy, coverage and evaluation¶

    chao_shen_entropy, nsb_entropy, cross_entropy, kl_divergence and perplexity.

    • Shannon, C. E. (1948). A mathematical theory of communication. Bell System Technical Journal, 27(3), 379–423. doi:10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb01338.x
    • Kullback, S., & Leibler, R. A. (1951). On information and sufficiency. The Annals of Mathematical Statistics, 22(1), 79–86. doi:10.1214/aoms/1177729694
    • Chao, A., & Shen, T.-J. (2003). Nonparametric estimation of Shannon's index of diversity when there are unseen species in sample. Environmental and Ecological Statistics, 10(4), 429–443. doi:10.1023/A:1026096204727
    • Nemenman, I., Shafee, F., & Bialek, W. (2002). Entropy and inference, revisited. In Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 14 (pp. 471–478). MIT Press. arXiv:physics/0108025
    • Wolpert, D. H., & Wolf, D. R. (1995). Estimating functions of probability distributions from a finite set of samples. Physical Review E, 52(6), 6841–6854. doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.52.6841 (the posterior-mean entropy used inside the NSB estimator)

    Methods without a separate reference¶

    MLE, Uniform and Random are standard baselines. CertaintyDegree is an experimental method with no published reference; its behaviour is documented on its method page.

    Software¶

    FreqProb's estimators are checked against NLTK's implementations, and FreqProb is built on NumPy and SciPy.

    • Bird, S., Klein, E., & Loper, E. (2009). Natural Language Processing with Python. Sebastopol, CA: O'Reilly Media.
    • Harris, C. R., Millman, K. J., van der Walt, S. J., et al. (2020). Array programming with NumPy. Nature, 585(7825), 357–362. doi:10.1038/s41586-020-2649-2
    • Virtanen, P., Gommers, R., Oliphant, T. E., et al. (2020). SciPy 1.0: Fundamental algorithms for scientific computing in Python. Nature Methods, 17(3), 261–272. doi:10.1038/s41592-019-0686-2
